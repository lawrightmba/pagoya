Phone and device signals. Device stability is one of the strongest non-traditional predictors in the alternative credit scoring literature (Tala, Branch, Lenddo all lean on this) — same device used consistently over time, versus frequent device or SIM swaps, which correlates with financial instability. Battery charging patterns and time-of-day usage rhythms have been used as proxies for routine and stability. Contact list size and call/SMS frequency (not content, just metadata) has shown predictive value in markets like Kenya and the Philippines, on the theory that a denser, more stable social-professional network correlates with repayment. For PagoYa specifically, WhatsApp message responsiveness to Paula — how quickly someone replies, whether they reply during business hours versus 3am, whether they ignore reminders versus engage — is a much more available and directly relevant version of this same signal.
Website and app engagement. You're already tracking session timing per your GA4 data; the next layer is consistency rather than just volume. Logging in or checking balance at a similar time each day or week is a routine signal — routine correlates with financial discipline more than raw engagement does. Worth tracking time between bill due date and when someone checks the app (early checkers vs. last-minute scramblers), how someone navigates after a missed or near-missed payment (do they immediately check their balance, or go dark), and whether they voluntarily browse financial literacy content versus only showing up when prompted. Search or click behavior on finance-adjacent content (your Module 1–5 curriculum, FAQ pages, the trust-copy block) is a good intent signal you can already capture without new infrastructure.
Beyond on-time/late for bill payment itself. A few that go deeper than the binary: payment timing relative to due date (paying 3 days early consistently vs. paying exactly on the due date vs. paying within a grace window) is a stronger trust signal than just "on time," since it shows margin and planning. Payment amount consistency — does the person pay the same biller the same amount each cycle, or is it erratic — flags either irregular income or irregular discipline. Recovery behavior after a missed payment (how fast they catch up, whether they proactively message Paula to explain or reschedule versus going silent) is one of the more predictive non-binary signals in microfinance literature, since it separates "temporary cash crunch with intent to pay" from "default risk." Use of partial payments or payment splitting, if you support it, is also informative. And cross-biller behavior — someone who reliably pays CFE but not water, versus someone consistent across all billers — gives you a more granular risk profile than a single aggregate.
BNPL-specific. Early repayment rate on installments is a strong positive signal. Utilization pattern — does someone use BNPL only when there's an actual liquidity gap (predictable, e.g. before payday) versus using it impulsively or for non-essential timing — is something you can detect from the gap between income-adjacent dates (if you can infer payday cycles from CFE/payment timing patterns) and BNPL usage. Whether someone pays down an existing BNPL balance before initiating a new one is a classic revolving-credit discipline signal. Decline-and-retry behavior (applying for BNPL, getting declined, behavior afterward) also carries signal: does the person try to game it repeatedly, or do they accept and engage with the financial literacy path instead.
Social and communication engagement (Paula-specific). This is probably your highest-leverage underused surface, since Paula already has the full conversational thread per user. Sentiment and engagement quality in messages to Paula — proactive questions about saving or budgeting versus only transactional "pay my bill" requests — signals financial engagement depth. Completion rate and pacing through your Module 1–5 curriculum (binge-completing vs. steady weekly cadence vs. abandoning) is a good discipline proxy. Voluntary engagement with the reward ladder mechanics (checking PTI score progress, asking about next tier) signals goal-orientation. Referral behavior — does the person refer others, and do those referrals also perform well — is a strong but underused social-graph signal; Lenddo built an entire model around the idea that your network's creditworthiness predicts yours. Response latency and tone consistency to Paula's nudge messages versus payment reminders is also worth separating out, since "responds fast to friendly nudges but ghosts payment reminders" is a distinct risk pattern from someone who's just generally slow to respond.
Composite/derived metrics worth building rather than tracking raw. Volatility scores (variance in payment timing or amount over a rolling window) often predict better than point-in-time status. Trend direction (is PTI-relevant behavior improving or degrading over the last 30/60/90 days) matters more than the absolute level for a young user. A "routine score" combining login time variance, payment timing variance, and Paula response time variance into one composite could become a clean new P-tier signal alongside your existing P0/P1 behavioral set. Given you already have the audit table and Mustache-templated messaging infrastructure, most of these are additive fields rather than new systems — the main lift is instrumenting the consistency/variance layer on top of what you're already logging as discrete events.
