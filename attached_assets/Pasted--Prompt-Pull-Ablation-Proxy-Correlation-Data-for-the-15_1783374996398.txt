CRITICAL METHODOLOGICAL REQUIREMENT — document the synthesis assumption per field, and flag circularity risk: for each of the 15 fields, before reporting its ablation delta, state explicitly HOW you chose to couple it to the existing latent factors (e.g., "minBalanceBuffer30d synthesized as a positive function of reliability with independent noise, weakly coupled to _ses to test whether it's genuinely separable from income-like signal" vs. "sequencingStability synthesized as directly proportional to reliability with no noise" — the second is a much weaker basis for trusting a high ablation number than the first, since a field synthesized as a near-deterministic function of the risk latent will trivially show strong ablation lift regardless of whether that reflects anything real). Rate each field's synthesis assumption as STRONG (loosely coupled, meaningful noise, plausible real-world mechanism), MODERATE, or WEAK (tightly/directly coupled to the latent, high circularity risk) in an explicit column, and treat WEAK-rated fields' ablation numbers as much lower-confidence evidence — good for ruling a field out (non-monotonic, unstable, redundant) but not for ranking it in.

Monotonicity check: does the field's relationship with the synthetic population's designed risk cohort move in a single, sensible direction (e.g., does higher minBalanceBuffer30d monotonically associate with lower designed-risk, with no reversal in the middle of the distribution)? A field whose apparent predictive power depends heavily on which synthetic cohort you look at is less trustworthy than one that's stable across all of them.
