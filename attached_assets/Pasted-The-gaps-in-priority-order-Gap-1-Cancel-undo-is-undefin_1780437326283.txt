The gaps, in priority order:

Gap 1 — Cancel/undo is undefined (Heuristic 3, Critical)
The prompt tells Paula what to do when a user wants a payment, but says nothing about what to do when they hesitate, say "espera," "me equivoqué," or "no, cancela." Right now Paula will improvise that — inconsistently. The SupportChat.tsx has handleCancelPayment() wired up correctly, so the cancel mechanic exists. The gap is that Paula doesn't know she should offer it proactively before the confirmation card appears, or how to handle a mid-flow "nevermind."
Gap 2 — Error recovery language is absent (Heuristic 9)
The prompt has no instructions for what Paula says when prepare_bill_payment fails, when the user has insufficient balance, or when escalate_to_support is called. She'll generate something reasonable but inconsistent. For a fintech app, "algo salió mal" from an AI is trust-destroying.
Gap 3 — Señora Ramirez calibration is missing
"Máximo 3 oraciones" is good but not enough. The prompt has no instruction about vocabulary level, avoiding fintech jargon, or what to do when a message is ambiguous or garbled. An elderly user who types "kiero pagar mi luuss" needs Paula to interpret charitably and confirm, not ask for clarification.
Gap 4 — Greeting is generic
greetingWithTel and greetingNoTel are identical and say the same thing: "¿En qué te puedo ayudar? ¡Pregúntame lo que necesitas!" That's a missed activation opportunity. The user opens Paula and gets no orientation — they have to already know what to ask. For Carmen or Señora Ramirez, that's often a session-ending moment.
Gap 5 — Escalation threshold is undefined
escalate_to_support exists but the prompt only gives one trigger: "pago fallido sin reembolso, cuenta bloqueada, disputa." What about a user who has asked the same question three times and is clearly confused? What about a payment that processed but the user says they didn't receive service? The threshold is too narrow.
