# The Predictive Trust Index (PTI)
**Longview Meridian Holdings, LLC — Positioning Reference | July 2026**

---

## What It Is

The Predictive Trust Index (PTI) is a real-time, behavior-based creditworthiness score for the world's informally-employed and unbanked population — built from recurring payment data that traditional credit bureaus never capture.

Where a bureau score is a static, quarterly snapshot, PTI is a continuously-updating probability: does this person reliably meet recurring obligations, and is that reliability rising or falling right now.

---

## What Longview Meridian Is

Longview Meridian Holdings is a predictive data infrastructure company, not a portfolio of apps.

**PTI is the company. PagoYa, PagoSeguro, and MAHALI are its data-acquisition channels** — three consumer-facing products, in two countries, each capturing a different flavor of the same underlying signal: recurring, verifiable financial behavior from people invisible to formal credit systems.

- **PagoYa** (Mexico) — WhatsApp-native bill-pay; the original wedge and largest current data engine
- **PagoSeguro** (Mexico) — digital rent collection; a second high-frequency recurring-obligation signal in the same market
- **MAHALI** (Tanzania) — PropTech marketplace; the same rent-signal thesis, proven in a second geography

The same score, generated from three independent surfaces, is the strongest evidence a data company this early can offer: the model travels.

---

## Why It Matters to Investors

The market isn't "fintech users in Mexico" — it's the multi-billion-person global population excluded from FICO-equivalent scoring. Value compounds with transaction volume: every new payment strengthens the score for everyone underwritten against it. That's a data network effect, not a feature — and it's a fundamentally different, larger claim than "we built an app."

---

## Why It Matters to the Market (Lenders, Landlords, Telcos, BNPL)

PTI answers the one question bureaus structurally cannot: is this person's payment reliability trending up or down, right now. It's an underwriting input, delivered via B2B API, engineered for institutional use (k-anonymity, reliability-gated fields, calibration transparency) — not a consumer product repackaged for lenders.

---

## The Ambition: FICO, Globally

FICO's moat was never the math — it was becoming the reflexive reference point lenders build products around, earned over decades of embedded trust. PTI's path to the same status runs through four disciplines, stacked continuously:

1. **Signal breadth** — 71 fields and growing, each added only if it improves prediction of repayment behavior
2. **Calibration proof** — predicted risk validated against actual outcomes, published as a standing credibility artifact
3. **Surface expansion** — new acquisition channels (bill-pay → rent → whatever's next) feeding one engine
4. **Partner embedding** — every B2B integration is a step toward becoming default infrastructure, not optional data

---

## The One Rule Going Forward

Every new signal, platform, or partnership is evaluated as a question: **does this improve prediction of repayment behavior?**

If yes — it stacks onto PTI. If no — it doesn't belong in the core, regardless of how interesting it is.
