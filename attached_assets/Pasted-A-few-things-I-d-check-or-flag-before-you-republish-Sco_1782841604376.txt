A few things I'd check or flag before you republish:
Score continuity / migration. You went v3.0 → v4.0-behavioral with new dimension weights. Anyone with an existing PTI score from v3.0 will see a number shift on next compute that has nothing to do with their actual behavior — it's a methodology change. Worth confirming: do you have a migration/backfill plan so existing users don't see their score swing (especially anyone near a reward tier threshold like 70/85, or near the READY gate at 80)? If a user was at PTI 81 yesterday and drops to 76 today purely from reweighting, that's a trust-damaging UX moment for a product literally selling trust. I'd run a batch dry-run of v4.0 against your existing user base before flipping it live, and diff the distribution against v3.0 scores to see how many people cross a tier boundary purely from the model change.
Cold-start / sparse-data behavior. Several of these signals need history to mean anything — device_consistency_score needs multiple days observed, routine_score needs login variance over time, recovery_score is literally undefined until someone has had a 30+ day gap. What do new users get for these sub-components — zero, null, or some neutral default? If it's zero, you're effectively penalizing new users on dimensions they haven't had time to demonstrate, which could distort onboarding-stage PTI in a way that doesn't reflect real risk. Worth explicitly testing a brand-new account through compute-now and checking the breakdown doesn't tank their score on signals they have no data for yet.
Gaming surface. A few of these are more gameable than your old core payment-timing signals. self_initiated_ratio and advance_payment_days are both things a sophisticated bad actor (or B2B partner gaming the score for a client) could manipulate by paying artificially early on a couple of token bills while ignoring real risk elsewhere. Worth checking whether these are weighted/capped against payment volume so one or two early payments can't swing the sub-score disproportionately for low-activity users.
device_consistency_score and compliance. This is the one I'd flag against the LFPDPPP point from before — device fingerprinting tied to a trust/credit score is the kind of signal that draws extra scrutiny under Mexican data protection law and in your STP/B2B partner disclosures, since it's closer to surveillance-adjacent data than transactional history. Before this goes into a B2B-facing score, worth a quick gut-check with Julio on whether your privacy disclosures and consent flow (the existing READY handoff consent, or signup ToS) already cover device-level tracking for credit scoring purposes, or need an update.
Tests I'd run before republish, concretely:
Dry-run v4.0 against the full existing user base and diff against v3.0 scores — flag anyone crossing a reward tier or the READY (80) gate purely from reweighting.
Run compute-now on a synthetic brand-new account (zero history) and confirm the breakdown doesn't unfairly zero out behavioral sub-scores.
Spot-check recovery_score logic specifically for users who've never had a 30+ day gap — confirm it returns a neutral/null value, not a punitive default.
Confirm the financial_curiosity_index topic classifier isn't double-counting messages (e.g., a single Paula conversation touching both savings and PTI topics counted twice toward the ratio).
Load-test compute-now with the 7 new signals added, since you've gone from 12 to 19 total sub-signals — confirm the 2-min queue processor and DEAD-letter policy still hold under the added compute load if this runs at scale across your user base, not just single-record calls.
Verify pti_export_safe view doesn't leak device_consistency_score or other potentially sensitive new columns to B2B partners beyond what's disclosed in the updated B2B data card.
